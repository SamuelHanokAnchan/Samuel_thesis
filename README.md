## What this repository is

This repository accompanies a master's thesis that evaluates the matching engine of a deployed
apprenticeship platform. It contains:

- the **sanitised research implementation** — the evaluation metrics, the offline replica of the
  hard eligibility filters, the TF-IDF baseline, the feature engineering, and the learned re-ranker;
- two **runnable Jupyter notebooks**, one that walks through the matching pipeline on synthetic
  data and one that presents the experimental results and figures;
- the **saved result artefacts** (aggregate metric arrays) and the **thesis figures**;
- the **unit-test suite** (49 tests).

## What it is **not**

- It does **not** contain the production codebase of Mariposa-Edu GmbH, which is company
  intellectual property. Only the excerpts needed to document the method are reproduced (in the
  thesis appendix).
- It does **not** contain the evaluation dataset. That data consists of real records of
  identifiable young people and cannot be released. The `sample_data/` in this repository is
  small, **synthetic**, and exists only to make the pipeline runnable.
- Because the data cannot accompany the code, this repository lets a reader **inspect and audit
  the method**, but it does not let them regenerate the thesis numbers. That limitation is stated
  plainly in the thesis (§3.11).

## The research in one minute

Germany's dual vocational training system fails to match at scale: in 2023, 73,400 apprenticeship
places went unfilled while 26,400 applicants remained without a placement. Internationally educated
candidates are served worst by conventional matching, because a student who writes *"vehicle
mechanic"* and a vacancy titled *"Kfz-Mechatroniker"* share no word.

The study compares three ranking systems on a pseudonymised export of 320 candidate profiles, 75
vacancies and 692 real recruiter decisions:

1. **Semantic retrieval beats keyword matching** on real recruiter decisions (NDCG@5 0.187 vs
   0.077, *p* = 0.0004).
2. A learned **re-ranker reverses sign** depending on the label source: slightly worse than the
   deployed engine when judged against the platform's own logs (−0.023), substantially better when
   judged against labels the platform never influenced (+0.142). The logs were generated by the
   very system the re-ranker was trying to beat.
3. The platform's **hard filters exclude 95%** of the pairs recruiters actually acted on, mostly
   because students apply across cities.

The central methodological lesson generalises beyond apprenticeship matching: **a deployed
recommender's own interaction data will tend to confirm the deployed recommender.**

`02_pipeline_walkthrough.ipynb` to see how the engine works, then `01_results_and_figures.ipynb`
for what it found.

## The two notebooks

### `02_pipeline_walkthrough.ipynb` — how the engine works

Runs the complete matching pipeline on the synthetic sample data, one stage at a time:

1. **Stage 1 — hard filters.** Shows which of the four gates (city, start date, training track,
   language CEFR) rejects each vacancy for a student, and reproduces the filter-attrition finding
   in miniature.
2. **Stage 2 — semantic retrieval.** Flattens a profile into one paragraph, embeds it, and ranks
   vacancies by cosine similarity. Demonstrates the cross-lingual match (English *"vehicle
   mechanic"* → German *Kfz-Mechatroniker*) and, deliberately, the embedding's blindness to
   language proficiency.
3. **Stage 3 — learned re-ranker.** Builds the 10 features, trains a LightGBM LambdaRank model
   grouped by student, shows the feature importances, and re-orders a candidate list.

### `01_results_and_figures.ipynb` — what it found

Loads the saved result artefacts and regenerates the key comparison charts: semantic vs keyword,
the sign flip, and the feature-importance shift. Then presents the seven thesis figures, each with
a short note on what it demonstrates.

## Two things to know about the demonstration

**The embedding is a local stand-in.** In production, Stage 2 uses Google `gemini-embedding-001`
(768-dimensional) reached over an API, and the production embedding text depends on the proprietary
ESCO occupation catalogue. Neither is shipped here. So `src/demo_embedding.py` substitutes a small,
deterministic, offline embedder built only on scikit-learn. **The pipeline shape is identical**
(flatten → embed → L2-normalise → cosine k-NN); only the embedding model differs, and the demo
embedder is weaker than the production one. The two production-replica modules that cannot run
without those external resources (`text.py`, `embeddings.py`) are documented in the thesis appendix
rather than included here.

**The sample data is synthetic.** The ten students, eight vacancies and sixteen decisions in
`sample_data/` were written by hand to exercise the pipeline (cross-city applications, varied German
levels, cross-lingual interests). They are not real people and are not drawn from the evaluation
dataset.

## Reproducibility and data protection

Every number in the thesis is produced by code, from fixed random seeds, and written to a saved
artefact; the figures are generated from those artefacts rather than transcribed. The metric
implementations are covered by 49 unit tests whose expected values were computed by hand rather
than copied from the code's own output.

The study is therefore **internally reproducible and externally auditable, but not externally
reproducible**: the evaluation data are real records of identifiable young people and cannot be
released, and Stage 2 depends on a proprietary production embedding. A third party can read and
re-run this code and confirm the method behaves as described; they cannot regenerate the thesis
figures.
